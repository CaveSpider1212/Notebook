It is important to assess competition when running a business to make sure it survives and grows. It can be done in 3 steps:
1. Profile current competitors
2. Identify and profile potential competitors
3. Develop your competitive position

Also, competitive analysis is very important both when starting out the business and throughout its lifetime.

### Profile current competitors

Develop a basic profile of the current competitors (both online and offline).

First, analyze companies that are direct competitors, then answer the following questions:
- **What are their strengths?** - Price, service, convenience, and brand are some areas where our business could be vulnerable
- **What are their weaknesses?** - These are opportunities we could take advantage of
- **What are their basic objectives?** - What is it they're trying to achieve (gain market share, capture premium clients, etc.)
- **What are their marketing strategies?**
- **How can you capture market share from them?**
- **How will they respond when you enter the market?**

In order to gather this information, we can:
- **Analyze competitors' websites and marketing materials** - Important information about their products, services, prices, and company objectives (could be a weakness of theirs if this information isn't there)
- **Visit their locations (if they have an offline presence)** - Collect sales materials/promotional literature (and also ask friends to stop in or call)
- **Evaluate their marketing and advertising campaigns** - Tells us how a company positions itself, who it markets to, and what strategies it employs to reach potential customers
- **Browse** - Search news, public relations, and other discussion of the company as well as blogs and twitter feeds to see how customers perceive them (along with possible expansion plans or new markets/changes in management)

Some databases to gain insight on competitors and markets include:
- **IBISWorld**
- **Business Source Complete**
- **Factiva**
- **Mergent Intellect**

### Identify and profile potential competitors

To identify when and where new competition may arise, regularly search for news on the industry, our products, services, and target market.

Basically, if serving the market seems easy (high profit margins, entering is easy/inexpensive, market is growing, supply is low and demand is high, little competition), then we can safely assume competitors will enter the market.

### Develop your competitive position

Answer the following questions to develop your competitive position once you've profiled the current and potential competitors:
- **Who are my current competitors?**
	- What is their market share?
	- How successful are they?
- **Which market do current competitors target?**
	- Do they focus on a specific customer type, on serving the mass market, or on a particular niche?
- **Are competing businesses growing or scaling back on their operations?**
	- Why?
	- What does that mean for your business?
- **How will your company be different from the competition?**
	- What competitor weaknesses can you exploit?
	- What competitor strengths will you need to overcome to be successful?
- **What will you do if competitors drop out of the marketplace?**
	- What will you do to take advantage of the opportunity?
- **What will you do if new competitors enter the marketplace?**
	- How will you react to and overcome new challenges?

Besides helping us understand our competition, competitive analysis can help us identify changes we should make to our business strategies.
