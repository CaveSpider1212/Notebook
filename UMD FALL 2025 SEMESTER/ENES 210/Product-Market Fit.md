**Product-market fit** means being in a good market with a product that can satisfy that market.

The Lean Product Process is used to improve product-market fit and has 6 steps.

### Step 1: Determine your target customer

Use market segmentation to focus on your target customer.

Personas are a good way to describe your target customer. They are a precise definition of your target customer and what they wish to accomplish as represented by a fictional character that you create.

Use databases like IBIS World and Mergent Intellect to get insights on candidate customers.

### Step 2: Identify underserved customer needs

Understand the needs and wants of the target customers we determined, and identify those that correspond to a good market opportunity.

### Step 3: Define your value proposition

See [[Value Proposition]]

### Step 4: Specify your MVP feature set

Specify what features your minimum viable product (MVP) will include.

Build only what is needed to create enough value in the eyes of your target customer to validate that you are heading in the right direction.

Goal is to iterate until you have an MVP that customers agree is viable and that aligns with a pricing strategy that makes sense for you (customers may say the MVP is missing a feature or has an extra feature that may not be used).

### Step 5: Create your MVP prototype

It is cheaper to build an MVP prototype rather than a live, working version of the MVP. The prototype is a representation of your product that you create without having to build your actual product.

Prototypes can vary in fidelity (the level of detail to which they resemble the final product) and interactivity (the degree to which the user can interact with the prototype compared to the final product).

### Step 6: Test your MVP with customers

Get feedback from people that are in our target market (otherwise it could send us in the wrong direction).

Use screeners (short survey) to ensure we are reaching the target customer, and then talk to them.

Carefully observe what the customer says and does as they use the MVP, and ask clarifying question to develop deeper understanding.

Ask open-ended questions like "Could you please tell me what you thought of that feature?"

Conduct tests in batches or waves (about 5-8 users is best)

### Final Step: Iterate to improve product-market fit

If any of our hypotheses about feature set, value proposition, underserved customer needs, target customer base, or prototype need to change, then we need to go back to the respective step and make revisions.

Test with a new wave of target customers after revising the MVP prototype.

Once you repeat the whole process multiple times, that is when you should feel comfortable investing the resources required to build the product.
