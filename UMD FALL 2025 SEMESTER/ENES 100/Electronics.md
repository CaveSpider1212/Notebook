Electronics allows us to utilize electrical energy to power our vehicles and sensors.

All components are connected together in a **circuit**, which is a closed loop interconnection of electrical components (battery, resistors, switches, LEDs, etc.).

Circuits involve conductors (wires), an energy source (usually a battery), and several components (resistors, switches, loads, sensors, etc.).

**Programming** is writing a series of instructions in a programming language that allows a computer to perform a set of tasks.

An **Arduino** is a **microcontroller** that we can program which helps us talk to the electronics, telling them what to do.

In Arduinos, you connect wires from a numbered pin on the microcontroller to the breadboard, which may have resistors, LEDs, etc., and then another wire comes from the breadboard to one of the ground pins.

If we want the circuit to do multiple things (such as turn on multiple LED lights), we can connect wires into 3 numbered pins from the microcontroller to the breadboard, connect wires from the 3 LEDs to the "common ground" at the bottom allowing for a shared return path , and connect a wire from the common ground back to the ground pin.
