> [!info] Ideal Gas Law
> $$PV = nRT$$
> 
> $P$: pressure (atm)
> $V$: volume (L)
> $n$: # of moles (mol)
> $R$: constant ($8.206 \times 10^{-2} \frac{\text{L} \cdot \text{atm}}{\text{mol} \cdot \text{K}}$)
> $T$: temperature (K)
> 
> Boyle's Law = $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$

> [!tip] Kinetic Molecular Theory
> 1. Particles have negligible size relative to container
> 2. Particles have kinetic energy proportional to temperature
> 3. When particles collide, the collision is elastic

### Mixtures

$P = \frac{nRT}{V}, n_{\text{total}} = n_1 + n_2 + ...$
$P_{\text{total}} = \frac{n_1 RT}{V} + \frac{n_2 RT}{V} + ... = \frac{RT}{V} (n_1 + n_2 + ...)$

> [!info] Partial Pressure
> $$P_1 = \chi_1 P_{\text{total}}$$
> 
> $$\chi_1 = \frac{P_1}{P_{\text{total}}} = \frac{n_1}{n_{\text{total}}}$$

### Real Gases

> [!info]
> Volume is no longer negligible in real gases (in particular, when pressure is high).
> 
> $$V = \frac{nRT}{P} + nb$$
> 
> $V$: corrected volume
> $b$: constant

### Attractive Interactions

Particles must overcome attractive interactions (IMFs), so the pressure is lower than predicted by the Ideal Gas Law (meaning particles lose energy).

> [!info]
> $$P = P = \frac{nRT}{V} = a(\frac{n}{V})^2$$
> 
> $P$: corrected pressure
> $a$: constant

> [!info] Vander Walls Equation
> $$[P + a(\frac{n}{v})^2][V - nb] = nRT$$

### Yield

> [!example] Stoichiometry/Yield Question
> Consider the following reaction:
> $$4 KO_2(\text{s}) + 2CO_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow 2 K_2 CO_3 (\text{s}) + 3 O_2 (\text{g})$$
> 
> If 21.8 g of $K_2 CO_3$ is produced from 27.9 g $KO_2$ and 29.0 L $CO_2$, what is the theoretical and percent yield? The molar mass of $KO_2$ is 71.10 g/mol and the molar mass of $K_2 CO_3$ is 138.21 g/mol.
> 
> Find the limiting reactant using stoichiometry:
> $KO_2$: $\frac{27.9}{1} \times \frac{1}{71.10} \times \frac{2}{4} =$ 0.196 mol $K_2 CO_3$
> $CO_2$: $\frac{29.0}{1} \times \frac{1}{22.4} \times \frac{2}{2} =$ 1.29 mol $K_2 CO_3$
> 
> $KO_2$ is the limiting reactant since it produces less of the product $K_2 CO_3$, so we need to use the amount of product generated by the $KO_2$ when finding the theoretical yield.
> 
> Finding the theoretical yield:
> $\frac{0.196}{1} \times \frac{138.21}{1} =$ 27.1 g $K_2 CO_3$ <--- This is the theoretical yield
> 
> Finding the percent yield:
> $\left(\frac{21.8}{27.1}\right) \times 100 =$ 80.4% yield
