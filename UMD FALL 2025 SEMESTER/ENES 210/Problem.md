We can define problems by looking at the 4 U'S, if the problem is:
- **Urgent**: top priorities for companies/individuals
- **Underserved**: absence of viable solutions to the problem
- **Unworkable**: existing solutions to problems are broken/don't work well
- **Unavoidable**: fundamental requirement for accounting, compliance, and privacy

It is best if the problem is both conspicuous and critical (which stands in the way of business) rather than concealed and craving (unacknowledged and probably optional). However, craving problems could be successful as well.

### What problems excite startup accelerators?

Problems relating to breakthrough technology, internet, and mobile companies are typically the ones that interest startup accelerators
- Examples: AI, biotech, robotics, etc.
- More examples: Education, healthcare, improving democracy, etc.

### Is the problem worth solving?

Once we identify the problem and evaluate it using the 4 U'S, we need to determine if it is worth solving or not (i.e. are there enough customers that will pay money for us to solve their problem) which can be done with the following questions:
- Who is your customer?
- What are their needs and wants?
- Are there enough customers?

It is a good idea to talk to potential customers to understand what features and values matter to them to understand your market size and potential, as well as how much they are willing to pay for the product.

That way, we can characterize consumers into distinct groups with the following characteristics:
- Groups have a particular need which justifies the creation of a product to match this need
- Group needs a separate distribution channel in order to be reached
- Groups require relationships of different kinds
- Very clear difference in the level of profitability each group represents for the organization
- Each group feels strongly enough to pay for a different version of the product or service, tailored to meet their needs

### What is customer discovery?

Customer discovery is the process of finding the target customer segment. The goals are to validate (or invalidate) the problem and later validate the fit of the solution to that problem.

We can also begin to identify and test the minimum viable product (MVP).

Customer discovery is NOT:
- Asking friends/family if they like the solution
- Evangelizing your vision
- Pitching your solution
